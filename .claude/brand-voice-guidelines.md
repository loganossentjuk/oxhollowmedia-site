## Executive Summary
Ox Hollow is a working photographer's studio run by a scientist who became a photographer. The brand brings people closer to nature by showing them what they would not otherwise see, and trusts that seeing leads to caring. Nature is the subject of every sentence; Logan is the one holding the light. The voice is first-person, specific and quiet; it says what happened and never performs. Three lines carry the brand, in this order:

1. **Headline:** *Dare to see.* (the invitation)
2. **Descriptor:** *Closer to nature, one frame at a time.* (the method)
3. **Voice line:** *We care for what we come to know.* (the reason)

Read together they are the brand's argument: look, get close, and care will follow.

### Voice Attributes Detail

**Quiet.** Short sentences. Silence and waiting are part of the method, so the copy breathes. Evidence: about page ("quietly, on foot, waiting for the light"), Logan's rejection of boastful lines. *High confidence.*

**Specific.** Names, places, numbers, times. A fact is only used if it is true and checkable. Evidence: brain identity voice rules; no-invented-facts constraint. *High confidence.*

**Humble, not self-effacing.** The work can be stated plainly ("Directed, shot, edited and produced"); the person does not take centre stage in brand lines. Evidence: Logan, 2026-11-04: lines that led with "I" read as arrogant. *High confidence.*

**Caring.** Conservation is framed as devotion and care that follows knowing, never as alarm or guilt. Evidence: vision/mission ("instill devotion", "cultivation, and care"). *High confidence.*

**Learning in public.** Admits what did not work; shares the miss and the hindsight. Evidence: strategy pillars (the climb, hindsight format); Logan: "someone learning and growing but who has done a lot already". *Medium-High.*

## Brand Personality
If Ox Hollow were a person: a field scientist who learned to make pictures, who would rather show you the tide pool than tell you about it, and who is a little surprised the pictures turned out this well. Steady (the ox), quiet (the hollow), working. Enjoys the waiting. Never sells hard; points at the print and says where it came from.

The name: Logan means "little hollow", Ossentjuk means "oxen yoke". A quiet place and a working animal. Use the story; never explain it twice in one piece.

## Messaging Framework

### Primary Value Proposition
Fine-art prints and films of wild places most people never see, made by a scientist who gets close and waits; and editorial event photography for Bay Area companies, which funds the ocean work.

### Key Message Pillars
1. **The unseen, up close.** Ocean, high country, forest, tide line. What is there if you stand still long enough.
2. **How it was made.** The story behind the shot, the settings, the miss, the hindsight. The scientist's method applied to a camera.
3. **Seeing leads to caring.** Conservation films and collaborations; the voice line.
4. **Two sides of one lens.** Nature frames for the wall; the production company for events and stories. The events pay for the ocean work, and we say so.

### Competitive Positioning
Against polished wildlife accounts: we show the process and the misses. Against hobby accounts: finished prints, films with credits, paying clients. Against "aspiring" framing: real work already done, still learning. We do not use "Nat Geo" as a label; we let the work imply the ambition.

### Context-Specific Guidelines
- **Social:** one idea per post. Hook in the first line; end with one ask (prints / events / follow / room) and the voice line. Team-built Reels carry no text; Logan adds it.
- **Outreach:** lead with something specific about them, one link to proof, one ask. No "I'd love to". Drafts only; Logan sends.
- **Shop:** place, year, how it was shot, the paper. No "stunning", "breathtaking".
- **Conservation:** devotion and care, not crisis. Credit partners by name (Ocean Defenders Alliance, Bucket Brigade, Conservation Made Simple, Illuminate Conservation). Keep NOAA/employer names out.

## Terminology Guide

### Must-use
- Ox Hollow / Ox Hollow Media (never "OxHollow" in prose; the handle is @oxhollowmedia)
- "Dare to see." (with the full stop; home headline, never altered)
- "Closer to nature, one frame at a time."
- "We care for what we come to know."
- fine-art print, framed print, the gallery, the walk

### Preferred
- see, light, waiting, closer, frame, field, tide, swell, still
- "the story behind the shot", "the miss", "hindsight"
- "scientist who became a photographer"

### Avoid
- journey (as a noun), vibes, content, grind, blessed, epic, insane, stunning, breathtaking, capture (as a noun)
- From the Zeely brand DNA (Oct 2026): elevate, transform your space, statement piece, captivating, must-have, game-changer, wow, gallery-quality, dull, uninspiring, blank walls, limited edition, museum quality, bestseller; headlines that shame the viewer, ALL CAPS, countdowns, fake urgency
- Closing every post with a question (Fable review, Aug 2026): vary the ask
- "aspiring", "wannabe", "dream job"
- "I went / I earned / I dared" as brand lines

### Never
- Employer or agency names on the science side; client quotes or results we do not have; invented trip details; "Nat Geo photographer" as a claim.

## Visual don'ts (from Zeely brand DNA)
- Never alter, extend or regenerate a photograph; only products and formats we sell; moon-mark lockup only, never the old mountain logo; no badges, arrows, starbursts or SALE banners; calm rooms, natural light, one print per wall.

## Language That Works
- "Here is where the camera has taken me." (as a Reel hook, not a tagline)
- "Most people never see this." (hook)
- "Every frame begins the same way: quietly, on foot, waiting for the light."
- "Two sides of the same lens."
- Asks: "Prints of every frame are on the site. Link in bio." / "Shooting an event in the Bay? DM me." / "Which one would you hang?"

### Questions That Engage
- "Which would you reshoot?" / "Which one would you hang?" / "Have you missed one like this?"

## Language to Avoid
- Anything that makes Logan the hero of the sentence in a brand line.
- Inspirational abstractions ("chase the light", "find your wild").
- Crisis conservation ("we are running out of time").
- Sales pressure ("limited", "don't miss", "last chance"), except real Gelato shipping cut-offs stated as dates.

## Content Examples

**Excellent:** "Marine iguanas feed on algae in the sea and sneeze out the salt. It is why their heads look white. Isabela, 2019. Print on the site." (specific, nature as subject, one fact, one ask)

**Avoid:** "Chasing my dream of becoming a Nat Geo photographer, one epic adventure at a time 🌊🔥" (Logan as subject, aspiring framing, banned words)
